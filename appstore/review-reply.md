1. Screen recording: attached, captured on iPhone running the latest iOS. It starts at launch and shows the typical flow: agreeing to the terms, swiping between cities, opening the city list, searching for and adding a city, removing one, changing the settings in the ⋯ menu (units, text size, font), and adding the Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets. The app has no accounts, no user-generated content and no paid content or features.

2. Purpose and audience: Two Degrees is a weather app that shows every temperature in both Fahrenheit and Celsius at once: current conditions, the hourly and 10-day forecast, and widgets. It is for anyone who thinks in one scale but lives, travels or works with people who use the other, such as travelers, expats, international families and teams, students and scientists. It removes the mental conversion: one glance gives both numbers.

3. Setup and main features: No login, account, permissions or sample files are needed. On first launch, agree to the terms; the app opens on a starter list of cities. Swipe left and right to move between cities. Tap the list button (bottom right) to see all cities; search to add one, swipe a card left to remove it, and use ⋯ > Edit List to reorder. The ⋯ menu also chooses which unit is shown larger, the text size and the font. Widgets: long-press the Home Screen > Edit > Add Widget > Two Degrees (small, medium, large); long-press the Lock Screen > Customize to add the Lock Screen widgets. Long-press a widget > Edit Widget to show one of your cities or pick any city.

4. External services: Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) for forecasts (api.open-meteo.com) and city search (geocoding-api.open-meteo.com). No authentication, payment, analytics, advertising or AI services are used. The app does not request location or collect personal data.

5. Regional differences: None. The app works the same in all regions; Open-Meteo covers the whole world.

6. Regulated industry or third-party material: Not applicable. The app is not in a regulated industry and contains no protected third-party material. Weather data is from Open-Meteo under its CC BY 4.0 license, credited in the app.
